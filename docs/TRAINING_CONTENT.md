# Phishing Awareness Training Content

## 1. What is Phishing?

Phishing is a social-engineering attack in which an attacker pretends to be a trusted person, company, or service to trick a victim into taking an unsafe action.

The attacker may attempt to make the victim:

* Click a malicious or suspicious link
* Open an unexpected attachment
* Reveal login credentials
* Share sensitive information
* Transfer money

---

# 2. How Phishing Works

A typical phishing attack can follow these steps:

```text
Attacker
   ↓
Creates deceptive message
   ↓
Creates urgency, fear, curiosity or reward
   ↓
Sends message to victim
   ↓
Victim interacts with message
   ↓
Attacker attempts to obtain information or money
```

The attacker depends heavily on human trust and decision-making.

---

# 3. Common Types of Phishing

## Email Phishing

Deceptive emails designed to make users click links, open attachments, or provide information.

## Spear Phishing

A targeted phishing attack aimed at a particular person, organization, or group.

## Smishing

Phishing performed through SMS or text messages.

## Vishing

Phishing performed through voice calls.

## Clone Phishing

A deceptive copy or modification of a legitimate message.

## Fake Websites

Websites designed to look trustworthy while attempting to trick users into entering information or taking unsafe actions.

---

# 4. Recognizing Phishing Emails

Common warning signs include:

* Unexpected messages
* Urgent or threatening language
* Suspicious sender addresses
* Unexpected attachments
* Links that do not match the expected destination
* Requests for passwords or OTPs
* Requests for payment
* Unusual grammar or wording
* Requests to bypass normal procedures

---

# 5. Recognizing Fake Websites

Users should check:

* Website domain
* URL
* Spelling
* Unexpected login pages
* Website context
* Requests for sensitive information
* Inconsistent branding
* Unusual behavior

### Important

HTTPS alone does not prove that a website is legitimate.

A suspicious website can also use HTTPS.

---

# 6. Social Engineering

Social engineering is the manipulation of human behavior to encourage an unsafe action.

## Urgency

Attackers may create artificial deadlines.

Example:

> "Your account will be closed today."

## Fear

Attackers may threaten negative consequences.

Example:

> "Your account has been compromised."

## Authority

Attackers may pretend to be:

* Bank employees
* IT administrators
* Managers
* Government officials

## Curiosity

Attackers may use messages designed to make victims curious.

## Reward

Attackers may claim that the victim has won a prize or received a reward.

## Trust

Attackers may impersonate a person or organization that the victim already trusts.

---

# 7. Best Practices

To reduce phishing risk:

* Stop before clicking unexpected links.
* Check the sender.
* Check the destination URL.
* Verify unusual requests independently.
* Do not share passwords.
* Never share OTPs with unexpected requesters.
* Use multi-factor authentication.
* Use strong and unique passwords.
* Keep browsers and software updated.
* Use security and spam filters.
* Report suspicious messages.

---

# 8. What To Do After Clicking a Suspicious Link

If you clicked a suspicious link:

1. Stop interacting with the page.
2. Do not provide additional information.
3. Close the suspicious page.
4. If you entered credentials, change the affected password using the legitimate service.
5. Enable or verify multi-factor authentication.
6. Notify the appropriate IT/security team when applicable.
7. Monitor the affected account for suspicious activity.

If financial information was exposed, contact the relevant financial institution through its official contact method.

---

# 9. Phishing Safety Rule

## STOP → CHECK → VERIFY → REPORT

### STOP

Do not react immediately.

### CHECK

Check the sender, URL, request, and context.

### VERIFY

Use an independent trusted channel.

### REPORT

Report suspicious messages to the appropriate organization or security team.
